XPRS

eXtended Packet Radio System

XPRS is APRS carried forward: the same idea -- a small text packet, heard by everyone and repeated by volunteers, building a live map of who is where, doing what, needing what -- rebuilt with what three decades of running the original taught.

A packet is at most 250 bytes of key:value text, readable without a decoder:

t:message f:X1QZ3N d:LISBOA ts:2026-08-08_14:26:40 m:net starts in ten minutes

78 bytes. A person reads it, and so does a five-line parser. The same packet travels LoRa, Bluetooth, WiFi, HF/VHF/UHF, wired networks and the internet.

What the decades taught

  • Identity is proven, not asserted. Packets are signed with a key the station holds; a licensed callsign can be bound to a key and proven live on air.
  • Delivery is custody, not hope. Stations carry mail for the absent and hand it over with signed receipts; a station away for four days asks for the days it missed.
  • No central system. Where APRS has one internet backbone that sees every packet, XPRS has indexers each station chooses, federated by directories rather than by copying each other's traffic.
  • Files of any size. Content-addressed by SHA-256: described, found by asking who holds them, fetched in verified pieces, or carried inline when tiny.
  • One syntax with room to grow. Every value carries its unit, unknown keys are skipped without error, and adding a field never breaks a reader.

Who runs what

Every role is somebody's ordinary device, usually several roles at once: a station speaks under a callsign, a relay repeats what it hears, a carrier holds mail for the absent, a gateway bridges radio and internet and publishes who it hears, an indexer archives the publications of the stations that chose it. Nothing is appointed; everything is volunteered and bounded by its operator's budgets.

Licensing

No licence is needed on licence-free spectrum (LoRa and Bluetooth in the ISM bands, WiFi, the internet), with identity from a keypair generated on the device. On amateur bands XPRS operates under amateur rules: plain text, a government-issued callsign, no ciphertext.

Specification

  • XPRS.md -- the full specification, current edition XPRS-2026
  • xprs_corpus.json -- the conformance corpus: every worked example as a wire with its byte count and derived identifier; an implementation that replays all of them byte-exact reads the format
  • github.com/xprs-dev/spec -- the specification repository

Reference implementations

  • geograms/aurora -- Dart/Flutter host for Android, Linux and Windows: codec, signing, store-and-forward, the heard-traffic archive and history server, and the transports (BLE5, LoRa slots, Reticulum, TCP port 4242)
  • geograms/reticulum-dart -- the shared Dart networking library: mesh transport, DHT, content-addressed file swarm, short-Schnorr signatures
  • aurora/esp32 -- ESP32 C implementation: a T-Dongle as an XPRS station, digipeater and store-and-forward mailbox, replaying the same corpus as the Dart codec

Editions

The specification is edited continuously and released by year. XPRS-2026 is the document as it stands at any point during 2026; the first change made in a later year opens the next edition and freezes this one. A year with no changes produces no edition.

XPRS operates alongside APRS with respect for its rules: traffic from self-generated callsigns is never originated onto amateur infrastructure, and ciphertext never touches the amateur bands.
